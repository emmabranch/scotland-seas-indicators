Harbour and grey seals

August counts by Seal Management Unit across six national censuses, 1996-97 to 2021-25

Status
✕ Clear concern
Trend
↘ Declining
Confidence
●●● High
Period
1996-2025

What this measures

The number of harbour seals and grey seals counted hauled out on land during the harbour seal moult in August, from helicopter surveys by the Sea Mammal Research Unit (SMRU). The whole Scottish coastline is surveyed roughly every five years. Counts are reported for Scotland’s seven Seal Management Units (SMUs).

A count is not a population estimate. Many seals are at sea during any survey. Comparing counts across years works because surveys follow a consistent method at the same time of year.

Why it matters

Both species are protected under the Marine (Scotland) Act 2010 and are features of Special Areas of Conservation. Harbour seals in particular have declined sharply in parts of Scotland since the early 2000s, and the causes are not fully understood. Seal counts feed into OSPAR and UK Marine Strategy assessments of marine mammals.

Trend and status

The 2021 to 2025 census counted 19,978 harbour seals, the lowest Scottish total since the first full census in 1996-97 and 26% below 2016-19. It counted 16,042 grey seals in August, 37% below 2016-19.

Grey seals need extra care. August counts capture grey seals hauled out in summer, which is not the main way their population is monitored (that is through pup production in autumn). The report notes that the low 2021-25 grey seal total is driven mainly by a very low 2025 count in Orkney, so one survey has a big influence.

Confidence

High. These are systematic, repeated national surveys by a specialist team using consistent methods, and the declines are large relative to survey uncertainty in most units. Confidence is lower for:

  • Grey seals in August, as above.
  • Single-year counts in any one area, which can be affected by weather and disturbance on the survey day.

Limitations

  • Counts are a minimum, not a population size. The report scales the latest counts to population estimates with wide confidence intervals.
  • Census periods span several years, so the “midpoint” on the charts is a simplification.
  • Survey methods changed over time (for example, the move to thermal imaging), which the report describes.
  • The data describe change, not its causes. SCOS and SMRU continue to investigate potential drivers of harbour seal decline.
