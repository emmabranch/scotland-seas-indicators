What this measures

Each year the Scottish Government assesses the conservation status of 173 wild Atlantic salmon stocks: single rivers, or groups of rivers where catches cannot be separated. A stock’s status is the probability that it will meet its conservation limit (the number of spawning adults needed for a healthy population) over five years:

  • Good: at least an 80% probability. No extra management needed.
  • Moderate: a 60 to 80% probability. Catch-and-release strongly encouraged.
  • Poor: under a 60% probability. Mandatory catch-and-release for the season.

The grading for each season is based on the previous assessment, so the “2026” grading reflects the most recent data.

Why it matters

Atlantic salmon is a protected species under the Habitats Regulations, with 17 Special Areas of Conservation designated for it in Scotland. It moves between rivers and the open ocean, so its status reflects conditions in both freshwater and the sea. This indicator reports status only. It does not try to attribute change to any one pressure.

Trend and status

115 of 173 stocks (66%) were in poor conservation status for the 2026 season, up from 102 (59%) in 2021.

Confidence

High that the gradings are reported accurately, since they are the official assessments. Medium for reading a trend from them:

  • Six seasons is a short window.
  • Each grading already averages over five years, so successive seasons are not independent.
  • Stocks near a threshold can change category with small changes in the underlying probability.

Limitations

  • Status is categorical. It hides how close a stock is to a boundary.
  • Assessments rely mainly on rod catch data (plus counters where available), which is affected by fishing effort and river conditions, not only by fish numbers.
  • 8 fishery districts have no individual river data and are graded poor by default.
  • Some rivers take the lower grade of an overlapping SAC, so their grading may not reflect their own assessment.
  • Stock boundaries are not openly available, so the map shows river mouths rather than catchments.
  • National counts are the official published figures. River-level data, the transitions grid, and the map are my reconstruction from the published tables. That reconstruction gives 173 stocks and matches the official Good, Moderate, and Poor counts exactly for every season from 2021 to 2026. The check caught two quirks in the source tables: one stock (North Harris SAC) spans two fishery districts, and one row used a different dash in a grouped river name.
